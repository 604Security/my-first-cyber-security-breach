# My First Cyber Security Breach: Resources for Parents

You don't need to be a tech expert to keep your family safe online. This page collects practical resources to help
you guide your kids, set up their games and devices, protect your own accounts, and know what to do when a
breach letter arrives. It goes with the free picture book My First Cyber Security Breach, in which Senior
Analyst Megaspy and her owl partner Hoot find out what happened when the Byte Bandit broke into the school's big
computer.

The focus is Canada and British Columbia, with government and non-profit sources first.

## 1. Talk with your kids

The most important habit: kids tell a grown-up when something feels weird, and they never get in trouble for it.

### Hoot's White Hat Rules (from the book, page 13)

1. Feel weird or worried? Tell a trusted grown-up!
2. Don't chat with strangers in apps or games, just like in real life.
3. No real info in apps or games (name, birthday, address, school) unless a grown-up says OK.
4. Passwords are for you and your parents only (not even Hoot!).
5. A phishy message, text or call? Don't click, don't answer, don't give your name. Show a grown-up.
6. Use a made-up name online, called a handle, like Megaspy.

## 3. Protect yourself (and model it for your kids)

### Secure your accounts

- Turn on MFA with an authenticator app, not text-message codes, for email, banking and anything important.
  - [Get Cyber Safe: Multi-factor authentication](https://www.getcybersafe.gc.ca/en/secure-your-accounts/multi-factor-authentication)
  - [Canadian Centre for Cyber Security: Secure your accounts and devices with MFA (ITSAP.30.030)](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/secure-your-accounts-and-devices-multi-factor-authentication-itsap30030)
- Use a password manager, a different password everywhere, and passkeys where offered.
  - [Get Cyber Safe: Password managers](https://www.getcybersafe.gc.ca/en/secure-your-accounts/password-managers)
  - [Get Cyber Safe: Passphrases, passwords and PINs](https://www.getcybersafe.gc.ca/en/secure-your-accounts/passphrases-passwords-and-pins)
- Keep devices updated and locked. [Get Cyber Safe: Secure your devices](https://www.getcybersafe.gc.ca/en/secure-your-devices)
- [Get Cyber Safe: Secure your accounts (all topics)](https://www.getcybersafe.gc.ca/en/secure-your-accounts)

### Phishing comes by email, text and phone

- Don't use the number or link in the message. Hang up and call the number on the back of your card.
- Forward spam texts to 7726 (SPAM) on Canadian carriers. See [Telus: Report a spam message](https://www.telus.com/en/business/support/article/report-spam-texts-smartphone).
- [Competition Bureau: The Little Black Book of Scams (2nd edition)](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/little-black-book-scams-2nd-edition)

## 4. Got a breach letter? Start here

1. Take the free credit monitoring offered in the letter, and enrol before the deadline.
2. Put a fraud alert on your credit file with both bureaus. An alert at one doesn't carry over to the other.
   Equifax Canada 1-800-465-7166 · TransUnion Canada 1-800-663-9980
   - [Equifax Canada: How to place a fraud alert](https://www.equifax.ca/personal/education/identity/articles/-/learn/how-can-i-place-a-fraud-alert-on-my-equifax-credit-report/)
   - [TransUnion Canada: FAQ (fraud alerts)](https://www.transunion.ca/customer-support/faq)
3. Check your credit reports from both bureaus for accounts you didn't open.
   - [FCAC: Checking your credit report for errors and fraud](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/check-errors.html)
   - [FCAC: Protecting your financial information in the event of a data breach](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/protect-financial-information-data-breach.html)
4. Report fraud to the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://www.antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca)
   ([report online](https://www.antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/report-signalez-eng.htm), or call 1-888-495-8501), and to your
   local police (ask for a file number).
   - [Competition Bureau: How to report fraud and scams in Canada](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/fraud-and-scams/tips-and-advice/how-report-fraud-and-scams-canada)
5. Reassure your kids: it wasn't their fault, and the grown-ups are fixing it.

### Credit freezes in BC: Bill 28

Starting August 1, 2027, BC residents can get a free credit freeze and free security (fraud) alerts, plus
their credit report and score free once a month. This comes from BC Bill 28 (2025), the *Business Practices and
Consumer Protection Amendment Act (No. 2), 2025* (S.B.C. 2025, c. 31). Until then, use a fraud alert plus monitoring.
Quebec and Ontario already have credit freezes.
- [Bill 28 as passed (Third Reading, December 3, 2025)](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/bills/billscurrent/gov28-3_43rd1st), BC Laws
- [B.C. Regulations Bulletin No. 26, 2026 (in-force dates)](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/regulationbulletin/regulationbulletin/2026bull26)
- [BC Gov News (2026): Strengthening consumer protection in B.C.](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026AG0057-000915)
- [BC Gov News (2025): Stronger tools coming to protect people from credit fraud](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025AG0055-000969)
- [Consumer Protection BC](https://www.consumerprotectionbc.ca)

## Hoot's Cyber Words (read together)

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Authenticator App | An app on a phone that makes a new code every 30 seconds, so only you can finish logging in. |
| Backdoor | A hidden way into a computer that a bad guy leaves behind so he can sneak back in. |
| Black Hat | A bad hacker who breaks in to steal or cause trouble. Like the Byte Bandit. |
| Credit Freeze / Fraud Alert | Grown-up tools that stop bad guys from opening accounts in your name. |
| Data Breach | When a bad guy gets into a computer and takes information that's meant to be private. |
| Hacker | Someone who loves learning about technology and is really good at figuring out how computers work, and how to get into them. |
| Handle | A made-up name we use online to keep our real name private, like Megaspy and Hoot. |
| Legacy System | A really old computer that everyone's scared to touch. |
| MFA (Multi-Factor Authentication) | A second lock on your account: after your password, you also need something else, like a code from your phone. |
| Password Manager | A safe vault that remembers all your passwords for you. |
| Phishing | A trick message pretending to be someone you trust, so you'll click, answer or share. By text it's "smishing"; by phone call it's "vishing". |
| Ransomware | Bad software that locks up files and demands treasure to unlock them. |
| SOC Analyst | A computer guard who watches for trouble all day. That's Hoot's job! |
| Threat Actor | The grown-up name for a bad guy who attacks computers. |
| White Hat | A good hacker who finds problems so they can be fixed. White Hats work in cybersecurity, with jobs like Incident Responder, Cyber Threat Intelligence Analyst, Penetration Tester, SOC Analyst and Security Analyst. Like Megaspy and Hoot! |
